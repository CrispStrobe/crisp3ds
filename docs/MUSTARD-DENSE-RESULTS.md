# Mustard dense reconstruction: failed depth-support diagnostic

The first corrected identity-copy continuation (`mustard-sparse-masked-dense-002`) **did not produce an evaluable mesh**. The [producer failure evidence](../tests/evidence/mustard-sparse-masked-dense-failures.json) binds both failed continuations; a separate [read-only depth/neighbor diagnostic](../tests/evidence/mustard-dense-002-failure.json) records the sealed native log, sole depth map, and corresponding native mask. No reference mesh, held-out image, or supplied pose was used in reconstruction or this failure diagnosis. The frozen [whole-mesh benchmark protocol](MUSTARD-DENSE-BENCHMARK-PLAN.md) remains unrun for this candidate.

The runner copied all 48 training RGB images byte-identically, preserved repaired image poses and sparse observations, and converted the exact zero-distortion SIMPLE_RADIAL intrinsics to equivalent PINHOLE intrinsics (maximum checked projection difference `1.14e-13` pixel). Native OpenMVS then loaded 48 calibrated views and 939 sparse points, and all 48 passed its initial neighbor-selection stage. Its subsequent per-reference log reported **one reference with one usable paired view and 47 with none**. It computed just `depth0001.dmap`; fusion could not load the paired `depth0003.dmap` and ended with zero fused depths and points. This is a depth-support failure, not a successful sparse-to-dense result.

The sole depth map, for `NP3_000.jpg`, is valid at 1280×1024 with 20,460 positive depth pixels (range 5.7286–7.3548 in the arbitrary SfM scene units), all inside its native mask. The mask has 20,460 positive pixels, only **1.56%** of the full image. Thus the observed zero output was not caused by that mask making every depth invalid. This one view does not establish that other masks or poses would support dense reconstruction.

In pinned [OpenMVS v2.4.0 Scene.cpp](https://github.com/cdcseacave/openMVS/blob/v2.4.0/libs/MVS/Scene.cpp), initial sparse neighbor scores use common visible 3D points, depth/angle/scale weighting, and projected covered area. The [SceneDensify.cpp `InitViews` step](https://github.com/cdcseacave/openMVS/blob/v2.4.0/libs/MVS/SceneDensify.cpp) then retains a neighbor only if its area-weighted score is at least `max(best score × 0.03, 2.0)`; the [DepthMap.cpp defaults](https://github.com/cdcseacave/openMVS/blob/v2.4.0/libs/MVS/DepthMap.cpp) define those cutoffs. The nominal minimum shared-area parameter of 0.05 is not itself sufficient to explain this case: `FilterNeighborViews` applies that filter only while a reference has more than nine neighbor candidates under the default maximum of 12. Passing initial selection but yielding zero/one usable pairing is consistent with the later score floor; small projected object area, poor camera geometry, or both could lower that score. The log does not separate their contributions. Independent pose inspection also found implausibly close centers for some near-opposite turntable angles and an outlying camera/point extent; 48/48 registered views are therefore **not** a validated camera solution.

No threshold was changed based on this failure. A separately [frozen common-crop continuation](MUSTARD-CROP-PLAN.md) was subsequently run; its result is documented in [MUSTARD-CROP-RESULTS.md](MUSTARD-CROP-RESULTS.md). Simply lowering OpenMVS neighbor-score thresholds could fuse incompatible views. Neither continuation produced an eligible whole mesh or a geometry-quality result against the separate Google shape reference.
